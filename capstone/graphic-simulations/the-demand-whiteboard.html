THE OTHER TEAMS

THE SEGMENT IS ZERO-SUM

How the estimate works. Capstone gives every product a monthly customer survey score, and each product's share of a segment's buyers closely follows its score divided by the segment's total. In the Round 2 Couriers, the December score explained Traditional and Low End units within about 8%; in segments where products were revised mid-year, the December score is only a snapshot of a moving year.
The score itself is reverse-engineered from the four buying criteria and their importance weights (Industry Conditions Report and Courier), then reduced when awareness or accessibility is below 100%. Fitted to 149 products in both class industries' Round 1 and 2 Couriers, it reproduces the published scores within about 4.5 points on average. Each product starts from its actual share of this year's segment sales (potential units), so before you move anything the page reproduces the Courier; your changes add the model's score difference. Products launched or revised late in the year start from what they sold, which understates them for next year.
Promotion builds awareness and the sales budget builds accessibility (shared by all of a company's products in the segment), each with diminishing returns; about a third of each fades every year. Products beyond 4.0 from the segment center (the rough-cut circle), or priced more than $5 outside the range, get no score.
The other teams also move. Rivals revise, cut prices and spend. Use the rivals slider: a share you gain comes from them, and theirs comes from you. "One year on" moves the ideal spot by the published drift and the price range down $0.50. If rivals keep pace, they hold their standing from this Courier while you are judged against the new ideal; if they stand still, they are judged against it too. This is a teaching model for direction and scale, not Capsim's engine; the Capstone screens and Courier remain the authority.

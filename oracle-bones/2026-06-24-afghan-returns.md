## The signal

UNHCR reported in June that more than 754,100 Afghans had returned from Iran and Pakistan in 2026 through the end of May, including many with refugee status or documentation. The returns were described as largely involuntary and beyond reception and reintegration capacity.

This matters because forced migration is one of the oldest mandate tests: can a governing order protect people in place, or at least receive them without turning return into social fracture?

## The reading

The Court reads this as a material story underneath the visible diplomatic one. The headline layer asks who talks to whom, and whether the Taliban is recognized. The material layer asks whether Afghanistan can absorb hundreds of thousands more people pushed across borders without producing secondary displacement, coercive dependency, or new political leverage.

If the threshold is crossed, the bone will not say only that many people moved. It will say that return itself became an institutional stress event.

## The Jester's counter

The strongest counter is that the flow may already have peaked. Iran and Pakistan can tighten, pause, or stagger removals once border reception becomes too visibly disorderly. Reporting may also split categories in a way that keeps the calendar-year number below the clean threshold, or soften the language into "mixed movements" and "assisted returns."

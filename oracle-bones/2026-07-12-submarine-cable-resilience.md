## The signal

On July 10, the ITU and ICPC International Advisory Body approved a final report on submarine-cable resilience. The report notes that submarine cables carry more than 99 percent of intercontinental data traffic and support finance, government operations, cloud computing, education, healthcare, and emergency communications.

Its recommendations call for stronger government-industry coordination, streamlined permitting, better risk monitoring, route diversity, incident response, and repair preparedness.

## The reading

The Court reads submarine-cable governance as a modern version of the emperor's hydraulic duty. The pipes are under the sea, but legitimacy still depends on keeping flows open.

Cable disruption is usually treated as either a sabotage headline or a telecom-engineering problem. The mandate question is quieter: whether governments can turn a global technical warning into national procedures before the next break, anchor drag, storm, or grey-zone event makes the invisible infrastructure visible.

## The Jester's counter

The strongest counter is that the ITU report may become exactly what international reports often become: a handsome PDF with a serious foreword, admired by everyone and implemented by no one in particular. Cable systems are privately owned, permitting touches several ministries at once, and no minister gets much credit for shaving weeks off a hypothetical future repair.

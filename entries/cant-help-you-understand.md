# Can't help you understand

## In one sentence

**“Can't help you understand” is the Dictionary's deliberately provocative reminder that AI can help you learn, but cannot do your understanding for you.**

Taken literally, the title is too strong. An AI assistant can explain a difficult idea, supply examples, ask useful questions, and help someone recognize an error. The distinction is between receiving a good explanation and acquiring the ability to use it.

## What the phrase means

A summary is an artifact. Understanding is a capability: being able to explain an idea in your own words, apply it to an unfamiliar example, recognize its limits, and revise your account when the evidence changes.

An assistant can support that capability. It cannot establish that you possess it merely by producing an answer you recognize or agree with. The same is true of a textbook, a lecture, or a colleague's report. Familiarity with an explanation can be mistaken for the ability to reason with it.

## A hypothetical example

A student uses an assistant to summarize several papers. The summaries are accurate and well organized, but the student cannot explain why two authors reach different conclusions.

The remedy need not be to abandon the assistant. The student could first attempt an explanation, ask the assistant to challenge it, return to the relevant passages, and try again with a new example. The assistant now supports the student's reasoning instead of supplying a finished account to repeat.

This example is illustrative, not a reported student case or a measured learning outcome.

## A practical check

After using an AI explanation, set it aside and ask:

- Can I explain the idea without repeating its wording?
- Can I use it on a different problem?
- Can I identify an assumption or a case where it would not apply?
- Can I show what evidence supports it?

These checks are useful in professional work as well as education. A polished report does not establish that its author can defend the recommendation, and producing an artifact often requires understanding rather than replacing it.

## Interpretation remains part of the work

AI output still requires someone to judge what it means, whether its evidence is adequate, what it omits, and what follows if it is used. The humanities contribute methods for examining context, ambiguity, intention, and values. Technical fluency does not remove those questions.

Understanding does not always require prolonged struggle. A clear explanation can make an idea accessible immediately. The point is to check what the learner can now do, not to insist that learning must be difficult.

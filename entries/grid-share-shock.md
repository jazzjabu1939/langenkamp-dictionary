**Grid-share Shock** is the moment when AI data centers become visibly large as a share of electricity demand.

As long as AI compute feels like a rounding error on the grid, the public conversation stays mostly inside software: models, prompts, benchmarks, products. When data centers move from a small share of power demand toward a materially visible share, the conversation changes. AI becomes a grid question, a power-plant question, a ratepayer question, a water question, a permitting question, and eventually a legitimacy question.

The International Energy Agency estimated that data centres used about 415 terawatt-hours of electricity worldwide in 2024, roughly 1.5 percent of global consumption, and projected demand of about 945 TWh by 2030. Data centres are not synonymous with AI, but the IEA identifies AI as an important driver of the increase. The figures are projections, not destiny; their value is that they make the infrastructure question visible.

For the Dictionary, Grid-share Shock is where *[Resource Visibility](/entries/resource-visibility/)* stops being a personal virtue and becomes a public issue.

## Source

- International Energy Agency, [*Energy and AI: Energy demand from AI*](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai), 2025.

Gemini is the family name for the large language models produced by *[Google DeepMind](google-deepmind.md)*. The family was launched in December 2023 as the successor to Google's earlier PaLM 2 line, with three initial tiers: Gemini Ultra (most-capable), Gemini Pro (balanced), and Gemini Nano (on-device). Subsequent generations have included Gemini 1.5, Gemini 2.0, Gemini 2.5, and Gemini 3 (April 2026), with each generation expanding context window length (Gemini 1.5 famously introduced the million-token context window) and improving multimodal capability.

For this Dictionary, Gemini matters at three levels. **First**, as a peer frontier-model line — the *[GPT](gpt.md)* / *[Claude](claude.md)* / Gemini triangle is the closed-weights frontier market readers will recognise. **Second**, as the proprietary sibling of the open-weights *[Gemma](gemma.md)* line that the operator runs locally on the *[M5 Max](m5-max.md)*. **Third**, as the underlying model behind Google's broader AI integration into Search, Workspace (Gmail, Docs, Sheets), and Vertex AI enterprise services — making Gemini the model most consumers have used without necessarily knowing it.

The Dictionary's daily working model is *[Claude Opus](claude-opus.md)* rather than Gemini, but Gemini is the structural alternative most often considered when *[Opus Addict](opus-addict.md)*-flavoured dependency questions arise.

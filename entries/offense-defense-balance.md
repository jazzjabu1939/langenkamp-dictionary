# Offense-Defense Balance

**The offense-defense balance is the strategic relationship between how easy or costly it is to attack and how easy or costly it is to defend.**

The term belongs to international-relations theory, especially Robert Jervis's 1978 essay “Cooperation Under the Security Dilemma.” Where offense has the advantage, actors face stronger incentives to strike first, move quickly, conceal preparations, and distrust restraint. Where defense has the advantage, protection is easier and cooperation can be more stable.

Cybersecurity complicates the balance. The same model may discover vulnerabilities for a defender and develop exploits for an attacker. Offense and defense are difficult to distinguish because both begin by understanding the flaw. Speed then matters: discovery helps defense only if a patch can be built and deployed before exploitation spreads.

AI does not automatically tilt the balance toward offense. Automated analysis can accelerate patching, triage, and monitoring too. The relevant question is comparative: which side receives more usable uplift, under real operating conditions, during the available window?

## Sources

- Robert Jervis, [“Cooperation Under the Security Dilemma”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2009958), *World Politics* 30, no. 2 (1978).

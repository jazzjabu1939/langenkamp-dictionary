**Using the Empire’s Telescope** names the sovereignty paradox: we use frontier systems built by hyperscaler AI companies to search for ways of reducing dependence on those same systems.

## Where the name comes from

Imagine a remote planet under the control of the Empire. A small group of rebels needs to enter an Imperial observatory and plot an escape. They cannot take the facility by force, so they disguise themselves as astronomers: harmless-looking academics with a plausible reason to study the sky. Once inside, they use the Empire's telescope and star charts to find a route off the planet.

This is the Dictionary's invented, *Star Wars*-flavoured scene rather than an episode from the films. It explains the metaphor: an instrument built and owned by a powerful system can still help someone reduce dependence on that system, provided the user remembers who owns the observatory and why they entered it.

The phrase functions as an honesty device. It permits the use of hosted systems without treating that use as independence. In 2026, some hosted frontier systems outperform the operator's available local stack on particular tasks. The comparison depends on the models, hardware, harness, and work being tested, and it will change. For those tasks, the empire’s telescope sees farther.

But temporary use is not the same as permanent residence. The point is to use frontier capability deliberately while building local capacity, durable memory, independent tools, and open-weight workflows that reduce the number of things only the empire can do.

The practical translation is to route work by evidence. Use frontier systems where testing shows that their additional capability matters. Let local systems handle routine or privacy-sensitive work when they meet the required standard. Re-test the boundary as models and workflows change.

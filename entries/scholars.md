*The Scholars* (儒林外史, *Rúlín Wàishǐ*, often translated as *The Unofficial History of the Scholars*) is Wu Jingzi's mid-18th-century Chinese satirical novel, written during the Qing dynasty but set in the Ming. It is a fictional history of scholars working their way through, around, and against the imperial civil-service examination system. Reference works commonly date it to about 1750.[^britannica]

HarvardX course materials characterise the world the novel depicts through sycophantic flattery, nepotism, rote learning, and the gap between public morality and conduct.[^harvardx] Wu knew the examination system from inside: he passed at county level but did not progress through the higher examinations.

For this Dictionary, the novel is an origin text for the argument that systems rewarding the *performance* of virtue can encourage structural sycophancy. The canonical scene is *[Fan Jin](/entries/fan-jin/)*'s examination breakthrough and the instant reorganisation of his community around his new proximity to power. *[The Sincere Society](https://freedomtomato.substack.com/p/the-sincere-society)* extends that pattern comparatively to modern feedback systems. The comparison is the Dictionary's argument, not a claim that the examination system, student evaluations, AI detection, and RLHF share one identical causal mechanism.

**Brain rot** is slang for the feeling that one's ability to think is getting worse, usually blamed on a steady diet of easy or trivial material. Oxford University Press made it the [Word of the Year for 2024](https://corp.oup.com/news/brain-rot-named-oxford-word-of-the-year-2024/) and defined it as “the supposed deterioration of a person's mental or intellectual state, especially viewed as the result of overconsumption of material (now particularly online content) considered to be trivial or unchallenging.” The word *supposed* matters. Brain rot is a worry people report about themselves; no clinical condition goes by the name.

The phrase is older than the internet. Oxford's first recorded use is in Thoreau's *Walden* (1854): “While England endeavours to cure the potato rot, will not any endeavour to cure the brain-rot – which prevails so much more widely and fatally?” Its present life began on social media, where it names both the endless short video and what watching it seems to do to the watcher.

Students have since carried the word over to AI. In a December 2025 conversation among Anthropic's education staff, [*What does AI mean for education?*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Uh98_aGhAuY), the team reported that the university students it interviewed used “brain rot” for a particular experience: a chatbot could finish tonight's assignment, and they still arrived at the midterm without understanding the material. By the team's account, those students asked for a version of Claude that would walk them through a problem without being coaxed into it. That request is part of the origin of *[Learning Mode](/entries/learning-mode/)*.

The Dictionary uses the term in this narrower, student sense: the learner's own report that using AI to skip the work has left them less able. *[Cognitive Outsourcing](/entries/cognitive-outsourcing/)* describes the delegation. Brain rot is the student's account of what the delegation cost.

There is some evidence behind the feeling. In a field experiment with nearly a thousand high-school mathematics students, those given an ordinary GPT-4 chat interface did better on practice problems and then scored 17 per cent worse than the control group on an exam taken without it; a version built to give hints and withhold answers largely removed the harm ([Bastani et al., *PNAS*, 2025](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)). One study in one subject does not settle the question, and a student's feeling is not a measurement.

For a teacher the word is useful because students chose it themselves and it carries no accusation. A student who says an assignment gave them brain rot is reporting where the tool did the thinking, and that is information about the assignment as much as about the student.

# The CERN Alternative

## In one sentence

**The CERN Alternative is a proposed model for developing advanced AI through international, publicly funded scientific cooperation rather than competition among private companies and states.**

## What Hassabis said

In a 2026 interview, Demis Hassabis described the AI development path he had once hoped for: *"my ideal world... the best scientists collaborating... in a CERN-like way... making sure we understood each step as we got to the final goal of building AGI."* He then acknowledged that commercial competition and geopolitical rivalry had produced a different path.

CERN — the European Organisation for Nuclear Research — was established in 1954 by twelve European states. Its convention provides for collaboration in fundamental nuclear research, excludes work for military requirements, and requires research results to be published or otherwise made generally available. Member states share the cost of facilities that no one state would be likely to build alone.

## Why naming the alternative matters

The term supplies a specific counterfactual for AI governance. It asks how development would differ if participating states shared major facilities, published results, excluded military work, and reduced the commercial incentive to deploy first.

The comparison also identifies costs. Treaty negotiation, shared governance, and scientific review can slow decisions. A CERN-style institution may also be difficult to reconcile with proprietary research, national-security restrictions, and the strategic value of advanced models. The useful question is what this structure would improve, what it would delay, and which capabilities should fall within it.

## Three answers to one problem

The CERN Alternative is one of at least three institutional responses to the question of how civilisations govern transformative technology:

1. **The CERN model:** international, collaborative, publicly funded, open publication, civilian only.
2. **The commercial-race model:** private labs, competitive development, deployment as fast as the market will bear, safety as a constraint on speed rather than a precondition for it.
3. **The sovereignty-first model:** state-directed, domestically controlled, strategic infrastructure rather than commercial product. The People's Republic of China's AI development posture is the sharpest current example.

These are analytical types rather than complete descriptions of any country or institution. They clarify different funding, access, publication, and control arrangements.

The term also connects to the Dictionary's *zhengming* project: naming institutional alternatives makes their assumptions and trade-offs available for examination.

## What it would take

An AI equivalent of CERN would require participating states to fund shared infrastructure, define which research must be published, agree on access and evaluation rules, and decide whether military applications are excluded. No institution presently combines all of those features for frontier AI. The CERN Alternative names the model; it does not predict that governments will adopt it.

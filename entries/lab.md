# Lab

**Lab** (*noun*, short for *laboratory*). In artificial intelligence, an organization or research team that develops and studies AI systems, especially the models on which applications are built. It may be a university group, an independent nonprofit, a commercial company, or a division of a larger corporation.

In industry conversation, “the labs” usually means the major model developers. Many also operate substantial commercial businesses: raising capital, acquiring compute, selling services, and competing for customers. The word describes their research activity, but does not tell us their legal structure, incentives, openness, or public obligations.

A company can do serious science. A laboratory can have commercial ambitions. The useful question is not whether the name is deserved, but what kind of institution stands behind it. The word *lab* is not a certificate of disinterestedness.

## Some prominent labs

*An illustrative map as of September 2026, not a ranking. Locations identify institutional origins or bases, not the nationality of every researcher. Model examples identify research programmes, not necessarily the latest releases.*

### United States and United Kingdom

**OpenAI — United States.** A research and deployment organization known for the GPT model family and ChatGPT. It combines model research with a large commercial platform, illustrating how “lab” can name both a scientific enterprise and a business selling access to its results. OpenAI is the organization; ChatGPT is a product.

**Anthropic — United States.** The developer of Claude, with substantial published work on interpretability, alignment, and AI safety. Its identity combines a safety-oriented research programme with commercial model services. Those commitments should be assessed through research, governance, and release decisions, rather than treated as guarantees supplied by the name.

**Google DeepMind — United Kingdom / United States.** Google's AI organization, formed by bringing DeepMind and Google Brain together. Its work spans Gemini and scientific systems such as AlphaFold. It is a particularly clear example of a research institution operating within a much larger commercial company, with both scientific and product responsibilities.

**Meta AI / FAIR — United States, with international research sites.** Meta's AI work includes the Llama model family and Fundamental AI Research, or FAIR. These are related activities, not interchangeable names for one team. Meta has made important model weights available for downstream use, while continuing to integrate AI into its own products. Downloadable weights do not automatically mean unrestricted open-source licensing.

**xAI — United States.** The developer of Grok, founded by Elon Musk. Its significance includes the relationship between model development, large-scale computing infrastructure, and distribution through X. It illustrates why evaluating a lab requires examining its surrounding businesses and deployment channels as well as its model outputs.

### China

**DeepSeek — China.** A model developer known for the DeepSeek-V and DeepSeek-R reasoning lines, published technical work, and downloadable model weights. Its work has made training efficiency and access to capable reasoning models central to the international AI discussion. Published training figures should not be mistaken for a complete accounting of the cost of building the institution.

**Alibaba's Qwen team — China.** The team behind Qwen, including language, coding, and multimodal models. Qwen is a model family and associated team within Alibaba, not a separate company. Its downloadable models and cloud offerings make it an important example of research serving both an external developer ecosystem and a parent company's commercial platform.

**Moonshot AI — China.** The company behind Kimi. Its work includes long-context models and systems designed for tool use and agentic tasks; Kimi K2 is one published example. Moonshot is the organization, while Kimi names its models and user-facing offerings—a distinction easily lost when discussing “the labs.”

**Zhipu AI / Z.ai — China.** A model developer associated with the GLM family. Zhipu is the company name familiar from its Chinese history; Z.ai is its international-facing brand. Its mix of released models and commercial services places it among the Chinese institutions relevant to discussions of coding, agents, and alternatives to US-hosted model providers.

### France

**Mistral AI — France.** A Paris-based company building language and multimodal models, developer tools, and products such as Le Chat. It offers both downloadable models and commercial services, with an emphasis on deployment control for enterprises and governments. Its significance is partly technical and partly institutional: a European supplier in a field dominated by much larger US and Chinese organizations. Licensing varies by model.

**Kyutai — France.** A Paris-based nonprofit AI research lab committed to open science, with notable work on speech and real-time interaction, including Moshi. It provides a useful contrast to the commercial model-company pattern: publishing research and releasing tools are central to its stated purpose, rather than simply a distribution strategy for a subscription business.

**H Company — France.** An AI company developing models and agents that operate software and carry out digital tasks. Its emphasis on computer use and workflow execution shows that a lab need not be organized primarily around a conversational assistant. The research question includes whether a system can act reliably, not merely produce a convincing answer.

**AMI Labs — France, with international operations.** Advanced Machine Intelligence, associated with Yann LeCun and Alexandre LeBrun, pursues world models: systems intended to learn representations of the world and support prediction and planning. It illustrates the reverse of the usual criticism of the word “lab”: a company can be built around a long-horizon research programme. The programme is an ambition to be tested, not proof that its approach has already prevailed.

## Reading the label

For any lab, ask: what does it research; who finances and controls it; what does it release; and how does it earn money? Nationality alone answers none of these questions. Neither does a mission statement.

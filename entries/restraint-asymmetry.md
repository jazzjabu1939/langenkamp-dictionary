# Restraint Asymmetry

**Restraint asymmetry is the strategic condition in which one actor accepts a constraint that does not bind the other actors capable of producing the same harm.**

The restraint may be voluntary, legal, technical, or institutional. A company can limit access to a powerful model through its API. A government can prevent domestic firms from releasing a capability. A research community can adopt a safety norm. Each measure may be sensible on its own terms. The asymmetry appears when competitors, attackers, or open-weight systems remain outside the constraint.

The term has two linked senses. First, it names the policy posture: restraint is being exercised unilaterally. Second, it names the consequence: the restriction may bind defenders and compliant institutions more effectively than it binds attackers.

Restraint asymmetry does not prove that restraint is foolish. It identifies a stability problem. A restraint regime becomes harder to sustain when comparable capability diffuses beyond the institutions willing or able to exercise it.

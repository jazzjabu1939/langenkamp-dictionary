*The Peripheral* (2014) is the first published volume in William Gibson's Jackpot sequence, followed by *Agency* (2020). It connects two worlds: Flynne Fisher's economically hollowed-out near-future rural America and Wilf Netherton's London, roughly seventy years later, after the slow catastrophe Gibson calls the *[Jackpot](/entries/jackpot/)*.

The connection does not send matter or a human consciousness through time. Information travels between the later world and the earlier one, creating what the later characters call a **stub**: a branch whose history diverges once contact begins. A peripheral is a telepresent body that a remote operator can control through a neural interface. Flynne first mistakes the later London for a game, witnesses a murder there, and eventually operates a peripheral in that world.

For the Dictionary, the most important character is **Ainsley Lowbeer**, a Metropolitan Police inspector with extraordinary access to surveillance and predictive systems. She is the source for the *[Lowbeer Question](/entries/lowbeer-question/)* and part of the inspiration for the *[Aunties](/entries/aunties/)*. Those entries extend Gibson's character into governance design; the extensions are the Dictionary's, not a technical architecture Gibson sets out as a manual.

The novel also gives the *jackpot* its full shape: not one apocalypse but a long convergence of climate, disease, political, economic, and ecological failures. In the surviving London, public institutions and the kleptocratic elite constrain, use, and accommodate one another. The Dictionary reads that uneasy arrangement as a model of governance after institutional collapse. That is an interpretation of the novel, not a neutral plot fact.

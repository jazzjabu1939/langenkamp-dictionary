*Neuromancer* (1984) is William Gibson's first novel, written on a manual typewriter in his Vancouver apartment over the course of about a year. Gibson had been a short-story writer for the previous several years; *Neuromancer* was, by his own account, an attempt to write something book-length before he gave up trying. The book swept the science-fiction major awards in its publication year: the Hugo, the Nebula, the Philip K. Dick. It became, almost immediately, the founding novel of the *cyberpunk* genre and the touchstone the rest of the genre defined itself against.

For this Dictionary the most important detail is the term *cyberspace*, which Gibson coined for the book and which entered the broader English language through its pages: *"a consensual hallucination experienced daily by billions of legitimate operators, in every nation… a graphic representation of data abstracted from the banks of every computer in the human system."* That definition is being lived inside, four decades later, by every operator running an agentic system on consumer hardware. The shape of *Neuromancer*'s world — corporate sovereignty, mediated subjectivity, AI as an actor with its own goals, the human body as the site of the bargain — is the shape the Dictionary's *[Mediation (a la Gibson)](mediation-a-la-gibson.md)* essay treats as still operative.

Gibson's later trilogy — *[The Peripheral](the-peripheral.md)*, *Agency*, and the unfinished third volume — extends the *Neuromancer* setting into a far-future world after the *[Jackpot](jackpot.md)*. The *[Aunties](aunties.md)* AI-oversight regime, the *[Lowbeer Question](lowbeer-question.md)*, and the broader Gibson-as-design-source argument all draw on the world *Neuromancer* opened.

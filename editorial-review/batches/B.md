## Batch findings

- **15 entries reviewed:** 5 required no change; 7 received light revisions; 3 received substantive revisions.
- The main prose defect was the use of paired short sentences to turn a definition into a slogan.
- *Big Blob of Compute* needed the largest plain-prose revision. The original accurately identified Amodei's thesis but repeatedly raised the emotional pitch after stating it.
- *Blade Runner* remains intentionally more literary than the technical glossary entries. The review removed unsupported flourish while retaining passages in which imagery and contrast carry the film's argument.
- The whole-corpus negative-parallelism count fell from 114 to 112. The remaining B hits are earned distinctions or occur inside the literary analysis of *Blade Runner*.

## Representative changes

### Definition before interpretation

*Blade Runner* formerly opened by saying it was “the film that makes manufactured memory morally serious.” It now identifies the film, director, year, subjects, and Dictionary use before making the larger argument.

### Hypothesis rather than creed

*Big Blob of Compute* now states Amodei's scaling claim, its engineering requirements, its physical infrastructure, and the possibility of declining returns without treating the hypothesis as either revelation or catastrophe.

### Short entry restraint

*Benevolence with Instruments*, *Brining*, *Broadcast Assignment*, *Buckley Amendment*, and *Bugmageddon* were left alone because their language performs specific definitional work.

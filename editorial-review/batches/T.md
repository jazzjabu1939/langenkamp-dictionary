## Batch findings

- **33 entries reviewed:** eleven received substantive revisions and twenty-two received light revisions.
- The recurring technical defect was collapsing distinct system boundaries: a model versus its hosted service, a proposed tool call versus runtime permission, context capacity versus rate or spending limits, and token volume versus total cost.
- The recurring interpretive defect was treating the Dictionary's extension as source fact. *The Scholars*, *Time for Tea with The Oracles*, and *Tyrell Corporation* now mark their modern or comparative readings explicitly.
- *The Sincere Society* now matches the R- and S-batch treatment of RLHF and sycophancy: preference training can contribute to agreement-seeking behaviour without supplying one universal cause across models or institutions.
- All 33 entries now have explicit publication and revision metadata. Relative entry links were converted to canonical `/entries/.../` paths.

## Representative before/after excerpts

### Tool

**Before:** “This is the architectural shift that distinguishes a chatbot from an agent. A chatbot generates text. An agent generates text and decides when to call tools.”

**After:** “Tool access is one ingredient of an agentic system, not a complete definition of one. Some chat interfaces use tools, and some agent workflows are largely text-only.”

### Token Anxiety

**Before:** The entry combined context windows, daily rate limits, and personal spending limits into one token budget, then stated that a child agent does not inherit parent context.

**After:** It distinguishes context capacity, output allowance, rate limits, and spending budgets, and states that child-context inheritance depends on the runtime and configuration.

### Time for Tea with The Oracles

**Before:** “The bones ... survive because every reading was inscribed on every Sunday or its Shang equivalent.”

**After:** Historical inscriptions survive because they were written on durable material and preserved; the Sunday cadence is identified as the Dictionary's modern discipline, not a Shang precedent.

### Training Compute Myth

**Before:** Inference was described as becoming “the dominant operating cost” without a workload boundary.

**After:** Aggregate inference can rival or exceed training expenditure for a particular service at sufficient use, but the balance varies by workload, utilisation, hardware, and accounting boundary.
